# HistoryForge record pages

The person, building, and census pages each answer a different question. Start
with what a reader can learn from the record and keep the evidence one step away.

- **Person** — What do the sources tell us about this person's life? Lead with
  the cited census summary and any written biography. Give each visible census
  appearance a dated entry with place, age, work, and a link to the source.
  Arrange years side by side at wider widths and in one column on mobile.
  Show attached photographs in a grid below the census, then stack the
  narratives with each citation directly beneath its narrative.
  Expand the household in context and keep a comparison table for research
  across years. A single record reads as a snapshot.
- **Building** — Who lived here over time? Lead with the address, map or
  available photograph, and a short sourced account. Group the resident
  households by census year and keep property details within reach.
- **Census record** — What did the enumerator record that year? Lead with the
  year, address, and household context. Put links to the person, building, and
  census sheet near the snapshot and keep the full transcription easy to reach.

Start with the person layout using existing permission-filtered evidence.
Andrew W Aagaard (Ithaca person 167992) is a multi-year example. Henry
Abatemarco (person 197696) is a single-year example and links through the
1950 census to 102 Irving Place. Building and census summaries are follow-up work.
